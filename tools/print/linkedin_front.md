# Preface

This book started as a series of LinkedIn posts. Each post took one layer of the enterprise GenAI stack, from the foundation models at the bottom to evaluation at the top, and paired it with the control that makes it safe to run in a regulated firm [AJ].

The posts were short by design: one argument, one signal to measure, one picture. This book gives each of them the room a post cannot. Every chapter opens with the post as it was written, then goes behind it: the evidence, the trade-offs, what good looks like, the honest objections and the questions worth taking to your own team [AJ].

The view is deliberately narrow. It is written for people who must make GenAI work inside a firm that answers to regulators, auditors and clients: architects, technology and data leaders, risk and compliance teams, and the engineers who build the platform. If your firm is less regulated, most of the advice still holds; you will simply be able to move faster on some of it [AJ].

Two things run through every chapter. The first is a conviction: the products will change every quarter, so the architecture that lasts is the control plane around them, owned by the firm. The second is one worked example: an agent that drafts the monthly performance-attribution commentary for a multi-asset fund, with a portfolio manager approving every draft. It is generic and illustrative, and it grows a piece at a time as the chapters go on [AJ].

The research behind the series was an independent review of 140 products and the regulation around them, completed in October 2026. It was carried out with the help of an AI model made by Anthropic. Anthropic's products are treated like every other vendor's here, and wherever one is named an independent alternative sits beside it [AJ].

Facts carry footnotes with their sources and the date they were checked. Versions, prices, owners and regulatory dates move quickly; treat every fact as true on its date, and check it again before you rely on it [AJ].

# How to read this book

**Read in any order.** Each chapter stands on its own. Part II follows the stack from the bottom up, pairing each layer with its control; Part III puts the pieces together into decisions [AJ].

**Each chapter has the same shape** [AJ]:

| Section | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| The post | The argument in under three hundred words |
| The figure | The same argument as one picture |
| Behind the post | The evidence and the trade-offs |
| What good looks like | The signal to measure, and a starting target |
| In the worked example | The piece this chapter adds to the commentary agent |
| Objections worth taking seriously | The strongest counter-arguments, answered |
| Questions for your team | What to ask tomorrow |
| In one line | The chapter's point, if you remember nothing else |

**Follow the worked example.** The commentary agent is built one step per chapter. Appendix A lists every step, from the brief to sign-off [AJ].

**Footnotes.** A footnote marks each sourced fact. Verified facts come from primary sources such as vendor documentation, regulators and standards bodies; reported facts come from secondary sources; everything else is the author's judgement or recommendation [AJ].

**Dates.** The evidence is as of {evidence_date} [AJ].
