Search-tool extracts (allowed domain eur-lex.europa.eu and searches citing the EUR-Lex text):
- Article 71: 'This Regulation shall apply from 11 December 2027. However, Article 14 shall apply from 11 September 2026 and Chapter IV (Articles 35 to 51) shall apply from 11 June 2026.' The Regulation enters into force on the twentieth day after publication in the Official Journal. EUR-Lex consolidated version is dated 2024-11-20 (02024R2847-20241120).
- Article 14: manufacturers notify actively exploited vulnerabilities to the CSIRT designated as coordinator and to ENISA through the single reporting platform.
- Article 13(8) (as reported from the final text): 'Manufacturers shall determine the support period so that it reflects the length of time during which the product is expected to be in use ... Without prejudice to the second subparagraph, the support period shall be at least five years.' Where the product is expected to be in use for less than five years, the support period corresponds to the expected use time. The end date of the support period, 'including at least the month and the year', is clearly and understandably specified at the time of purchase. The Commission may adopt delegated acts specifying minimum support periods for specific product categories.
- Recitals: manufacturers identify and document components, including by drawing up a software bill of materials; 'Manufacturers should not be obliged to make the SBOM public.'
- Article 3(2): ''remote data processing' means data processing at a distance for which the software is designed and developed by the manufacturer, or under the responsibility of the manufacturer, and the absence of which would prevent the product with digital elements from performing one of its functions.' Recital 11: processing at a distance includes the situation where a mobile application requires access to an API or a database provided by means of a service developed by the manufacturer. Recital 12: 'Cloud solutions constitute remote data processing solutions within the meaning of this Regulation only if they meet the definition laid down in this Regulation.'
- Article 64(10): by way of exception, administrative fines do not apply to microenterprises or small enterprises for failure to meet the 24-hour early-warning deadlines in Article 14(2)(a) or 14(4)(a), nor to open-source software stewards for any infringement. Recital 120: as stewards are not subject to the same obligations as manufacturers, administrative fines do not apply to them.
- Article 64(2) top tier (reported by penalty guides; tier allocation not confirmed in the extract): up to EUR 15 000 000 or 2.5% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.
- Article 12 links products that are also high-risk AI systems under Article 6 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 to the AI Act Article 15 cybersecurity requirement (see E1-S008).
