Search-tool extract: SB26-189 'Automated Decision-Making Technology' repeals and reenacts the standards and requirements for the development and use of AI systems created in SB 24-205. Signed 14 May 2026; Chapter 131, 2026 session laws. Scope: covered automated decision-making technology used in consequential decisions, i.e. 'a decision that relates to an individual's access to, eligibility for, or compensation related to education, employment, housing, financial or lending services, insurance, health-care services, or essential government services and public benefits'. From 1 January 2027 developers give deployers 'technical documentation describing the covered ADMT's intended uses, categories of training data, known limitations, and instructions for appropriate use and human review'. Deployers give clear notice at the point of interaction and 'a plain language description of a covered ADMT's role within 30 days after the covered ADMT makes a consequential decision that results in an adverse outcome for the consumer'. Consumers may request correction of factually incorrect personal data and meaningful human review after an adverse outcome. Enforcement by the Attorney General; before 1 January 2030 a 60-day cure notice; no new private right of action. Effective dates differ by section: the Division of Real Estate summary lists certain sections taking effect 14 May 2026; the AG page says the new law's provisions go into effect 1 January 2027.
